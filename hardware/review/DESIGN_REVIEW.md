# Hardware design review, revision v0.6

Review of the draft schematic (`hardware/kicad`, generated by `tools/gen_kicad_schematic.py`) against the
documentation that could be read. It records what each circuit does, what was checked and against which
source, what was changed in this revision, and what stays open with the exact input it needs. It is the
engineering basis for the human review in [REVIEW_CHECKLIST.md](REVIEW_CHECKLIST.md); it does not replace it,
and nothing here is a measurement.

**Status of the design: engineering design ready for review, not fabrication-ready.** The schematic is
complete and checked for connectivity; the power stage cannot be rated (battery maximum voltage, motor
current), the gate-driver pinout cannot be verified (datasheet not accessible), and no footprint is assigned.

## 1. Gate driver: pinout and operating limits

**Circuit.** DRV8353S (candidate DRV8353SRTAR, WQFN-40) as a project symbol whose pin names and numbers are
placeholders; charge-pump and regulator capacitors "TYPICAL - verify"; nFAULT pulled up to +3V3 and routed to
TIM1_BKIN (PE15) and to PE1; ENABLE driven only by the AND gate.

**Checked.** The routing of nFAULT to BKIN and of ENABLE from the AND gate (consistency items PR1, PR4: PASS).
KiCad 8.0.9 has no DRV835x symbol (`Driver_Motor`, `Driver_FET` searched), so no secondary pin map exists to
cross-check against. The operating limits quoted elsewhere (VM 9-75 V, 80 V absolute maximum, VDRAIN 102 V)
are [S] and are not used for any decision.

**Open.** Pin numbers, the 40-pin package's thermal pad, every limit, IDRIVE/IDRIVE settings, VDS trip levels
and deglitch, the CSA gain table, and the input pull-downs: all from SLVSDY6x. The firmware does not configure
the driver and never sets DRV_ENABLE (`firmware/platform/stm32g4/src/main.c`). **No footprint may be assigned
to U401 before the pinout is renumbered from the datasheet.**

## 2. MOSFETs: ratings, losses, gate charge, drive

**Rating rules** (unchanged, [D]): V_DS >= 1.5 x V_bat,max (switching overshoot and regeneration share the
margin until the double-pulse test measures them); continuous current from the motor's continuous phase
current at the worst-case junction temperature; pulse current and SOA from the motor peak current; R_DS(on) at
the driver's gate voltage; gate charge Q_g and Q_gd against the DRV8353 IDRIVE source/sink settings.

**Losses.** `hardware/calculations/power_stage_envelope.md` and `tools/run_electrical_calcs.py` give loss and
junction temperature over phase current and bus voltage for four representative parts, from [S]
values. They rank candidates for a given drive cycle; they select nothing.

**Open.** V_bat,max (battery datasheet), motor peak and continuous current (motor datasheet), then the MOSFET
datasheets. With 48 V nominal only, no voltage class can be chosen: the maximum charged voltage of the pack
is not known (the chemistry, cell count and BMS limits are not reported).

## 3. DC link: capacitance, ripple current, transients

**Method** ([D], `power_stage_envelope.md`): capacitor RMS current from the phase RMS current and modulation
index (Kolar-Round expression); bulk capacitance from the allowed bus ripple at the PWM frequency and the
battery-cable inductance; voltage rating >= 1.25 x V_bat,max plus the measured switching overshoot; ceramic
capacitors per half-bridge for the switching loop. Transient assumptions to be replaced by the double-pulse
measurement (acceptance stage 7).

**Open.** All six capacitor positions and the bleeder are UNRESOLVED: they need V_bat,max and the motor phase
current, and the cable length from the battery for the ripple term.

## 4. Current sensing, bandwidth and ADC range

**Chain.** Low-side four-terminal shunts -> DRV8353 integrated amplifiers (referenced to +3V3A/VREF) ->
100 ohm / 470 pF (tau = 47 ns, -3 dB at 3.4 MHz) -> ADC1_IN1 (PA0), ADC2_IN2 (PA1), ADC3_IN4 (PE7) [ST pin map].

**Sampling (implemented).** TIM1 centre-aligned; low side centred on the counter valley; OC4REF rises one
timer tick (6.25 ns) before the valley and TRGO2 starts the three injected conversions [ST: TRGO2 and JEXTSEL
encodings from the LL headers]. Sample time 24.5 ADC cycles at 40 MHz = 0.61 us [A]; a phase is used only if its
low-side window, 1 - 2 (dead time + settle) f_pwm of the period, contains the sample (validator rule R15b,
`mc_lowside_sample_mask`). The 470 pF capacitor is much larger than the ADC sampling capacitance [R: a few pF],
so the charge kick-back is small; the filter settles in about 7 tau = 0.33 us, inside the sample time.

**ADC range.** 12 bits over VREF+ = +3V3A. Full-scale current I_fs = (headroom-limited output swing) /
(G_CSA x R_shunt); with a bidirectional amplifier centred on VREF/2 [R], I_fs is about 1.65 V / (G x R).
Rule 37 requires the full scale to cover the hardware trip with margin; the error budget is
`hardware/calculations/current_sense_error_budget.md`.

**Open.** CSA gain options, offset, settling time and output swing (DRV8353 datasheet); the shunt value from
the motor peak current; the ADC clock limit and the sampling-time table against source impedance (DS12288).
Whether the three conversions sample at the same instant is to be measured (acceptance stage 4).

## 5. Hardware overcurrent response and its limits

**What exists.** One hardware path: the DRV8353's drain-source voltage (VDS) monitors -> nFAULT -> TIM1_BKIN,
which clears the main output enable in hardware (PR4: PASS; filter 4 samples at 160 MHz = 25 ns).

**What it covers and what it does not** ([D] from the principle; values need the datasheet):

| Fault | VDS monitor | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Shoot-through, phase-to-phase or phase-to-ground short while a FET conducts | detected | trip current = V_DS,trip / R_DS(on)(T_j): R_DS(on) rises with temperature [R: roughly 1.5 to 2x at 125 degC], so the current threshold falls when hot and is uncertain when cold |
| Overload below the VDS threshold (stall, excessive torque command) | not detected | firmware: software overcurrent in the current interrupt (outputs off in the same interrupt, latched in the next slow step) and thermal derating |
| Current while all FETs are off (regeneration through the body diodes) | not detected | limited by the motor's back-EMF and the battery; the BMS and fuse act upstream |
| Battery-side short upstream of the bridge | not detected | fuse and BMS |
| A sample missed because the phase was outside its window | n/a | the software check uses only valid samples; the hardware path does not depend on sampling |

The software check depends on the firmware running and on valid samples, so it is not independent.

**Option identified, not implemented: internal comparators.** ST's pin database shows each phase-current pin
on a comparator's non-inverting input: PA0 = COMP3_INP, PA1 = COMP1_INP, PE7 = COMP4_INP [ST]. With an internal
DAC on the inverting input and the comparator outputs routed inside the MCU to TIM1's break input, a hardware
overcurrent trip independent of the gate driver is possible without any schematic change. Limits: one current
direction per phase (in a three-phase fault at least one phase current is positive, so detection is guaranteed
at twice the threshold in the worst split); the shunt only carries current while its low side conducts, so
blanking is needed (comparator blanking from a TIM1 channel, to be confirmed in RM0440); the threshold comes
from `inverter.hw_overcurrent_trip_A`, the CSA gain and polarity. **Decision for the review** once the motor
peak current is known: internal comparators (firmware only), an external comparator on a bus or phase shunt,
or the VDS monitor alone.

## 6. Over- and undervoltage

**Over-voltage (hardware).** Bus divider -> TLV7041 `-` input; TL431 2.495 V reference -> `+` input; the
open-drain output `BRK2_N` with a 10 k pull-up goes to TIM1_BKIN2 (PC3), active low (PR5: PASS). Pin functions
from [K]: TLV7041DBV pin 3 `+`, pin 4 `-`, pin 1 open-collector output; TL431DBZ pin 1 K, 2 REF, 3 A. Normal
operation: divider below the reference, output released, BRK2_N high; over-voltage: output low, outputs off.

**Defect found and fixed.** The hysteresis resistor went from `BRK2_N` straight to the TL431 node. That node is
held by the shunt regulator's low dynamic impedance, so the resistor gave almost no hysteresis until it drew
more than the regulator's spare current, when the reference itself would collapse: the hysteresis was not
defined. Now the comparator `+` input (`OV_REF_IN`) is fed through 10 k from the TL431, and 390 k from `BRK2_N`
sets the hysteresis [D]:

* tripped (output low): V+ = 2.495 x 390/400 = 2.433 V, i.e. 2.5 % below the reference;
* released (output pulled up through 10 k): about 2.0 uA flows from the pull-up into the node, V+ = 2.515 V,
  0.8 % above.

So the trip is 0.8 % above, and the release 2.5 % below, the value set by the divider: about 3.3 % hysteresis,
whatever trip voltage the divider later sets.

**Reference bias.** The 1 k gave (3.3 - 2.495) / 1 k = 0.8 mA of cathode current. The TL431's minimum cathode
current for regulation is [R] up to about 1 mA, so the reference might not regulate. 470 ohm gives 1.7 mA
(1.4 mW in the resistor). Confirm against the TI datasheet.

**Trip value.** The divider ratio is 2.495 V / V_OV,trip; V_OV,trip comes from V_bat,max and the MOSFET and
capacitor ratings: open. The same divider feeds the ADC (`VBUS_SENSE` through 1 k), whose full scale is then
3.3 / 2.495 = 1.32 x V_OV,trip, which meets rule 37 (full scale >= 1.05 x the trip).

**Undervoltage.** Firmware only (`limits.undervoltage_trip_V`, derate at `limits.undervoltage_derate_V`) plus
the LM5164 enable/UVLO divider for the logic supply; both values come from V_bat,min and the BMS cut-off: open.

## 7. Temperature sensing and thermal assumptions

**Circuit.** 10 k NTC from `TFET_N` to ground near the low-side MOSFETs, 10 k bias from +3V3A, 1 k / 100 nF
filter to PE10 (ADC3_IN14); motor sensor input on J701 with an UNRESOLVED bias (sensor type unknown). The ADC
reference is the same +3V3A, so the reading is ratiometric.

**Firmware.** `mc_ntc_temp_degC`: beta model from `sensing.fet_ntc_*`; a reading within 1 % of either rail is an
open or shorted sensor and reported out of range; a missing calibration reports the temperature missing,
which the core treats with its unknown-temperature derate.

**Open.** The NTC part (R25, beta, tolerance), its thermal coupling to the MOSFETs (layout), the MOSFET
junction-to-case and case-to-heatsink resistances, and the motor sensor type. The trip and derate
temperatures stay null until they exist.

## 8. Precharge, bypass, discharge and failure detection

**Circuit.** Battery -> fuse -> TVS -> reverse-polarity block -> bus shunt (INA228) -> precharge resistor -> DC
link, with a P-channel bypass across the resistor. The bypass gate is held off by 100 k, clamped by a 12 V
Zener and pulled down through 47 k by a small N-FET from `PRECHG_EN` (100 k pull-down: open while the MCU is in
reset). The INA228 measures the battery side; `VBUS_SENSE` the link side. Discharge: a passive bleeder only.

**Firmware (implemented, host-tested).** `mc_precharge`: waits until the battery side exceeds the undervoltage
trip; closes the bypass when link >= `power.precharge_complete_fraction` x battery; a fault if that does not
happen within `power.precharge_timeout_s`, or if after closing the link stays below the fraction for longer
than the timeout (bypass not conducting, or an overload); loss of either measurement keeps the bypass closed
(opening it under load would put the load current through the resistor) but withdraws the gate-driver
permission. A fault opens the bypass and latches until a fault-reset. In this revision the battery-side meter is
not read, so the bypass never closes.

**Start-up order (`firmware/include/semmc/mc_startup.h`, host-tested).** The link charges through the resistor as
soon as the battery is connected; the MCU only closes the bypass. The DRV8353 is supplied from the link (VM =
VDC), so it is woken only after the bypass has closed, then configured and read back, and the current offsets
are measured with its amplifiers running. Before closing, the link reading must agree with the link voltage the
battery-side current implies, |V_DC - (V_BAT - I_BAT x R_pre)| <= `power.precharge_agreement_V`; this rejects a
stuck or offset reading on either side, which the voltage ratio alone accepts. The INA228 provides both V_BAT
and I_BAT, so a fault common to that one device is not covered by this check.

**Energy.** Each charge from 0 V puts E = 0.5 C_link V_BAT^2 into the resistor (equal to the energy stored in the
link) with an initial peak power V_BAT^2 / R_pre; `models/inverter/losses.py` `precharge()` computes both. C_link,
R_pre and V_bat,max are not selected, so no number is given. A controller reset does not repeat this energy:
the bypass opens in reset but the link stays charged through the resistor.

**Shorted link.** A short on the link side (a failed bridge leg, a damaged capacitor) holds V_BAT across the
resistor and dissipates V_BAT^2 / R_pre for as long as the battery stays connected. The MCU cannot interrupt this
current. The resistor must survive it until the vehicle isolates the battery, or the path needs an element that
opens (a fuse or PTC sized for it). This is a selection decision that waits for V_bat,max and R_pre.

**Not detected.** A shorted bypass or a missing precharge resistor (the link charges too fast): detection needs
the expected RC time, i.e. the resistor and the link capacitance. The bleeder's discharge time is not
monitored.

**Open.** Resistor value and pulse energy 0.5 C V^2 per attempt; bypass FET V_DS and SOA; Zener current
(V_bat,max - 12 V) / 47 k; bleeder value against the required discharge time; whether the precharge belongs on
this board or in the vehicle's contactor box (open decision 4 in `hardware/selection/COMPONENT_SELECTION.md`). All need V_bat,max and
the link capacitance.

## 9. Gate-enable defaults, reset and watchdog

**Verified by the netlist checks.** Every enable (`MCU_DRV_EN`, `DRV_EN_GATED`, `HW_PERMIT`, `PRECHG_EN`) has a
pull-down (PR3); ENABLE = MCU_DRV_EN AND HW_PERMIT through the 74LVC1G08, whose output has its own pull-down
(PR1, PR2); the MAX6369 output resets the MCU (0 ohm option) and pulls `HW_PERMIT` low through a BAT54 (D803)
whose cathode is on WDO (PR6; D_Schottky pin 1 = K [K]); `HW_PERMIT` is driven only by the safety-loop optocoupler (PR7).

**Changed.** The six 100 k pull-downs on the gate-signal inputs are fitted: during reset and until TIM1 is
configured the MCU pins are high impedance, and the DRV8353's own input pull-downs are not verified. With ENABLE
held low the driver should not switch anyway; the resistors make the inputs defined in every case. They may
become DNP once the datasheet confirms internal pull-downs.

**Firmware sequence (implemented).** At reset all pins are analog; `main()` first sets every output to its safe
level (gate signals as GPIO low, DRV_ENABLE low, PRECHG_EN low), then starts the IWDG, then the clock. TIM1 is
configured with the outputs off before its pins are switched to the timer. Fault handlers force the safe state
and stop kicking the watchdogs.

**Watchdog timing.** The MAX6369 straps are SET2..SET0 = 0, 1, 0 (pins 6, 5, 4 to GND, +3V3, GND). The timeout
and start-up delay for that code come from the Analog Devices table: open. Requirements on the choice [D]:
the timeout must exceed the longest gap between WDI edges, which is 1 ms in operation and up to 20 ms during the
start-up offset measurement (`measure_offsets`); the start-up delay must cover the time from reset to the first
kick, which includes the crystal start-up. The IWDG times out after about 100 ms at the LSI's nominal 32 kHz;
its tolerance is in DS12288 (open).

## 10. Emergency isolation interface

The vehicle's emergency stop isolates the battery upstream of J101 (vehicle hardware, not this board). The
safety loop (emergency stop and dead-man) drives the PC817 LED; its transistor drives `HW_PERMIT` high only
while the loop is energised (emitter follower, 10 k to GND). `HW_PERMIT` gates the driver enable in hardware and
is sensed by the MCU (PD2). Logic power comes from `VBAT_PROT`, so an isolated battery also removes the logic
supply. **Open:** the loop voltage and current (the LED series resistor, PC817 forward current [R: about 5 mA]),
the connector, and detection of an optocoupler that fails on (no check exists; the firmware still sees the
dead-man input separately).

## 11. DC-DC: input range, start-up, load transients

**Circuit.** LM5164 buck from `VBAT_PROT` (before the precharge, so the MCU runs while the link charges) to
+5 V; TLV75533 LDO from +5 V to +3V3; ferrite to +3V3A. The candidate is a 100 V input-class part [S].

**Loads.** CAN transceiver, hall sensors, pedal and controls supply (polyfused), the LDO (MCU, INA228,
comparator, logic). **The LTE modem is not on this rail:** the telematics unit has its own protected DC-DC from
the battery (procurement line "Protected DC-DC supply for the telemetry unit"), sized for the modem's peaks of up
to 2 A at 3.4-4.2 V (A7672X Hardware Design V1.04 p. 26 [P]). A shared rail would put those peaks on the
controller's analog supply; the interface specification keeps them separate.

**Open.** UVLO divider (V_bat,min), RON (on-time against the input range), inductor, input capacitors' voltage
rating (V_bat,max), and the 5 V current budget once the hall and pedal sensors are known.

## 12. CAN: transceiver, termination, protection, connector

TJA1051T/3: VCC from +5 V, VIO from +3V3, S pulled low (normal mode); pin functions [K] (1 TXD, 4 RXD, 5 VIO,
6 CANL, 7 CANH, 8 S). NUP2105L across CANH/CANL to ground ([K] description: dual-line CAN protector, 24 V
working voltage). Split termination 2 x 60.4 ohm + 4.7 nF behind two open solder jumpers, closed only if this
node is a bus end. Shield to ground through 1 nF. **Recommendation for layout:** a footprint for a common-mode
choke with a 0 ohm option, to be decided after the first bus measurement. **Open:** the bus bit rate and
format, the harness connector and pinout, and whether this node terminates the bus.

## 13. Clock source and peripheral timing

**Firmware (implemented).** SYSCLK 160 MHz from the PLL (input /4 = 4 MHz, VCO x80 = 320 MHz, /2), from HSI16 or
a 16 MHz crystal (`SEMMC_HSE_HZ`). PLL input and VCO are inside both ranges found in ST's sources (input
2.66-8 or 2.66-16 MHz, VCO 64-344 or 96-344 MHz; the conflict is recorded in DATASHEET_REGISTER.md). Flash
latency 4 wait states with range-1 boost, AHB /2 during the switch [ST: HAL]. Timers at 160 MHz (dead-time step
6.25 ns), ADC at 40 MHz [A], 1 MHz time base.

**Why CAN needs the crystal** ([D], bit-timing tolerance conditions of ISO 11898-1): with the proposed
500 kbit/s timing on a 16 MHz kernel (32 time quanta, phase segment 2 = 4, SJW = 4) the oscillator tolerance
allowed per node is the smaller of SJW / (20 x 32) = 0.625 % and min(PS1, PS2) / (2 (13 x 32 - PS2)) =
4 / 824 = 0.49 %. The internal RC's tolerance is [R] about 1 % over temperature, above that limit; a crystal's
is tens of ppm. The firmware therefore keeps CAN off without a crystal.

**Proposed decision.** 16 MHz crystal: exact classic CAN bit rates of 125 k, 250 k, 500 k and 1 Mbit/s with the
FDCAN kernel on HSE, and 160 MHz SYSCLK (`hardware/timing/clock_tree_alternatives.md`). It becomes a decision
when the vehicle bus is confirmed as classic CAN at one of those rates; the load capacitors then follow from the
chosen crystal's C_L.

## ERC warnings (CI run 39070286030 on 76f4cfc, KiCad 8.0.9: 0 errors, 9 warnings)

All nine were `lib_symbol_issues`: "symbol has been modified in library". Each was compared field by field
between the copy embedded in the schematic (KiCad 7.0.11 library, the release installed where the schematic was
last generated) and the KiCad 8.0.9 library the ERC used:

| Warning | Instances | Difference found | Electrical effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| power:PWR_FLAG | #FLG101-106 | pin name `pwr` -> `~`; same number, type (power_out) and position | none (ERC marker, not a component) |
| Reference_Voltage:TL431DBZ | U202 | two polylines of the drawing redrawn; pins identical | none |
| MCU_ST_STM32G4:STM32G474VETx | U801 | body enlarged and 46 pins moved by 2.54 mm; all 100 pin numbers, names and types identical | none on connectivity (the schematic uses its embedded copy) |
| Power_Protection:NUP2105L | D1101 | datasheet URL http -> https; drawing and pins identical | none |

Fields renamed between the releases (`ki_description` -> `Description`, new `exclude_from_sim` flag) appear on
every symbol and did not raise warnings, so the ERC does not compare them.

**Resolution.** The schematic is now generated from a pinned copy of the KiCad 8.0.9 libraries
(`tools/fetch_kicad_symbols.py`, SHA-256 checked), and the ERC compares against the same copy
(`tools/run_kicad_erc.sh`), so embedded and library symbols are the same release. **Result:** CI run
39078886365 on commit 40b3dfa (schematic unchanged since a9a378c), KiCad 8.0.9: **0 errors, 0 warnings**
([docs/VERIFICATION_REPORT.md](../../docs/VERIFICATION_REPORT.md) section 8). A clean ERC checks schematic rules
only; it does not close any design-review item above.
