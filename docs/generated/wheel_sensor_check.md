## Result

A DRV5032 (any variant in SLVSDC7F) sees a wheel magnet only by chance at vehicle speeds. In the reference case it sees 11 to 17 % of passes at 10 km/h (t_S max to typ), and fewer at higher speed. Across the grid, a 20 Hz version is certain to see every pass only below 0.3 to 11.5 km/h. Counting pulses with it would under-read wheel speed by a speed-dependent, random factor, so it is **not suitable for wheel speed**. The datasheet lists "Battery-critical position sensing", tamper detection, case sensing and valve position among its applications (SLVSDC7F p. 1); none is a rotating-wheel count. A continuous Hall switch such as the DRV5023-Q1 responds in at most 25 us and sees every pass with margin at any speed in the grid. That part is a comparison read from its datasheet, not a selection: its supply current (2.7 mA typ), unipolar south-pole operation, open-drain output, mounting and the vehicle's maximum speed must be checked first.
