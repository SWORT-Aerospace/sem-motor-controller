## Reading

* Grade is the dominant error. A 0.1 % slope (1 m in 1 km) adds m g sin(theta), close to 40 % of this vehicle's rolling resistance, and the fit books all of it as rolling resistance. Driving the same section in both directions and averaging removes a constant grade to first order.
* A steady wind shifts the aerodynamic term; both directions also cancel most of it.
* Speed noise matters through the numerical derivative. The fit reports when A and C are no longer separately identifiable instead of returning a confident split.
* Requirements for the real coast-down: both directions on the same flat section, wind recorded, speed from the wheel sensor (GNSS as a cross-check), the vehicle's mass and rotating inertia, and the freewheel state. The inertia sets m_eff; this study uses the model's own value.
